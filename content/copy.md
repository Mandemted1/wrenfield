## The concept

Wrenfield is a 40-acre estate between Rhinebeck and Red Hook with four event spaces: a restored 1892 dairy barn, a glass pavilion, an orchard, and a small stone dining room. Weddings, corporate offsites, private dinners.

Two structural decisions carry the whole build.

**One:** the table-layout diagrams become a live planner. Set a guest count, pick an event type, and the floor plan redraws with the right number of tables, tells you which spaces fit, and gives a real cost range. "Can you fit 140?" is the first question in every venue enquiry and currently takes two days to answer by email.

**Two:** we publish what a wedding actually costs, itemised, on one real example. Venue pricing is the most deliberately misleading in the events industry — the advertised number is the site fee and the real number is three to five times larger. Bush Loerie already gestures at this with their "not included" list. We go all the way.

## Section 06 — Table layouts

Static reference diagrams, sage line art on bone, exactly the reference's treatment. Clicking one loads it into the planner above.

**Headline:** `TABLE LAYOUTS`

**Round tables** — Tables of 10. The most floor space per guest and the easiest for conversation. Everybody can see the room.

**Long tables** — Parallel runs of 20. Fits about 25% more people than rounds in the same footprint and looks better in photographs.

**Herringbone** — A head table with angled arms off a central spine. Works when you want a visible top table without a formal seating hierarchy.

**Theatre rows** — Chairs in rows facing one point. Ceremonies and presentations. The densest layout we set.

**Classroom** — Rows of tables facing forward. Conferences where people need to write.

**Boardroom** — One table, everyone around it. Up to 22 in the Stone Room.

**Horseshoe** — Open-ended U. Discussion-led sessions and small ceremonies where you want people facing each other.

**Standing** — High-tops scattered, no fixed seating. Cocktail receptions and drinks before dinner.

## Section 08 — What a wedding here actually costs

The most important section on the site. Dark forest field, bone type.

**Eyebrow:** ○ THE PART EVERYONE HIDES

**Headline:**
```
OUR SITE FEE IS $16,000.
YOUR WEDDING WILL COST ABOUT $74,000.
```

**Body:**
Every venue in this valley advertises the first number and lets you discover the second one in month four of planning. We think that's a rotten way to treat people who are about to spend a year's savings.

So here is a real wedding we hosted last September. 120 guests, Saturday, ceremony in the Orchard, dinner in the Barn. Every line, including the ones that are easy to forget.

### The itemisation

| | |
|---|---|
| Site fee — Saturday, September | $16,000 |
| Catering — 120 × $205 | $24,600 |
| Bar — 120 × $88, full open bar, 5 hours | $10,560 |
| Ceremony setup — chairs, arch, sound, rain plan | $2,500 |
| Chiavari chair upgrade — 120 × $14 | $1,680 |
| **Subtotal** | **$55,340** |
| Service charge — 22% | $12,175 |
| **Subtotal** | **$67,515** |
| NY sales tax — 8.125% | $5,486 |
| Overtime — 1 hour past midnight | $1,200 |
| **TOTAL** | **$74,201** |
| | |
| **Per guest** | **$618** |

### Three notes

**The service charge is the line that gets people.** Twenty-two percent on food and beverage is standard across this industry, and on a $35,000 F&B spend that's $7,700 you didn't budget for. It is not a gratuity; it goes to the house. Ask every venue you tour what theirs is, and ask before you fall in love with the room.

**What isn't in that number.** Photography, flowers, music, dress, rings, hair, invitations, transport, officiant, and the rehearsal dinner. Couples in this valley typically spend another $30,000 to $50,000 on those. Your all-in is closer to $110,000 than $74,000, and you should hear that from us in month one rather than month nine.

**How to spend less here.** A Friday in October is $9,000 instead of $16,000. Eighty guests instead of 120 takes roughly $22,000 off. Beer, wine and one cocktail instead of a full open bar saves about $3,400. Long tables instead of rounds means you can seat the same people in the Glasshouse, which is a smaller site fee. We'll walk you through all of it and we won't sulk about it.

## Section 12 — How to book

Four steps on a sage field, the reference's pattern but with real content instead of lorem ipsum.

**Headline:** `HOW BOOKING WORKS`

**01 — Check the date**
Tell us the date or the month. We'll tell you within one business day whether it's free. No form-filling required to get that answer.

**02 — Come and walk it**
Ninety minutes, any day of the week, free. Bring whoever's helping you decide. We'll show you all four spaces and give you a written estimate with real numbers before you leave. Nobody will ask you to commit on the day.

**03 — Hold it**
A 25% deposit holds the date. Fully refundable for fourteen days after you pay it, no questions and no conversation about it.

**04 — Plan it**
You get a named coordinator from the day you book. Three planning sessions are included, plus a walkthrough four weeks out and the rehearsal. Balance is due thirty days before.

## Section 14 — Questions

Accordion on a sage field, botanical line art bleeding off the right edge. The reference's FAQ treatment.

**Headline:** `QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS`

**Can we bring our own caterer?**
Yes. We have three we recommend and we take no commission on any of them. An outside caterer needs $2m liability insurance and a kitchen walkthrough at least thirty days out. We've never turned one down.

**What's the rain plan?**
The Glasshouse, which seats 150 for a ceremony and is four minutes from the Orchard. There's no extra charge and we make the call together by 10am on the day. Every couple who's had to use it has told us afterwards it was fine.

**What time does the music have to stop?**
Amplified music outdoors ends at 10pm, which is a local ordinance and not ours. Inside the Barn it runs until midnight, and past that it's $1,200 an hour. Most people buy one hour.

**Is there a noise limit inside?**
No. The Barn is 400 feet from the nearest neighbour and they're our cousins.

**Can we have fireworks or sparklers?**
Sparklers yes, in the gravel courtyard, with a water bucket we provide. Fireworks no, ever. The orchard is 80 years old and the fire risk between June and September is real.

**Do you host more than one wedding a weekend?**
No, and we won't start. One event, Friday noon to Sunday noon.

**What happens if we need to cancel?**
Full refund within fourteen days of the deposit. After that the deposit is non-refundable but transferable to any other available date within eighteen months. We'll work with you. We have never kept a deposit from someone in genuine trouble.

**Is the estate accessible?**
The Barn, Glasshouse and Stone Room are step-free with accessible bathrooms. The Orchard is a grass slope and firm underfoot when dry — we have a gravel path and golf-cart transport for anyone who needs it. Two farmhouse rooms are on the ground floor. Tell us what you need and we'll tell you honestly what works.

**Can we get married on a Tuesday?**
Yes, and it's $6,500. Weekday weddings are our favourite thing and nobody books them.
